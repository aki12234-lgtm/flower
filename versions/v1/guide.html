Botanical kinds

The twenty-eight parts a node can be, every one shown on the same specimen so only the botany differs. Eighteen are flowers or fruit, ten are foliage. Four — spider, burr, saw, arrow — are deliberately sharp where the rest are curved, and three — button, trumpet, pom — are deliberately plain. Which of them a plant grows is weighted by the hidden values, and only the strongest three are ever in play at once.

Materials

Forty-one states on one armature: twenty-two substances and nineteen graphic ones — prints, drawings, instrument readings.

Places

The six places a game can happen in — real ones, each with its own ground, its own weather at the horizon, and its own things growing or lying on it. A field has grass and hedges under open sky. A beach has the sea a little way out, shelving into it. The ocean is not beside the water but under it: the plant grows up off a reef through coral and fish towards a surface that is a ceiling of light. A forest is trunks on every side and almost no sky. A cave is rock on every side and no sky at all, lit by one break in the roof — which is also the only direction out of it. Which one a plant gets is fixed by its seed and holds for the whole game: it is the only part of the world that never changes under it.

Eras

The world keeps its own hours. Every so often the whole of it — ground, air, cloud, and the growth in the distance — takes on one manner, and the plant goes on standing in it in whatever manner the plant is in. The clock is time alone: not the material, not the answers, not how far anything has grown. About half of any session is the world as it is; the rest is one of these.
